Who this is for

A good fit

Self-funded employers with 100 or more employees, stop-loss in place, and a plan that can carry an advocacy and balance-bill program.

Usually not a fit

Small and level-funded groups. Their carrier or program usually sets the pricing, so there is no real choice to make here.

What makes RBP work

The percentage matters, but the support behind it matters more. Before any group moves to RBP, make sure the program includes:

  • Balance-bill handling. Someone works every bill a member receives until it is resolved.
  • Legal defense with no time limit. If a provider sends a member to collections or court, the member is defended for as long as it takes.
  • Direct contracts. Agreements with key local hospitals and doctors. Contracts, not higher percentages, are what stop balance bills.
  • Member education. Members learn not to pay a balance bill and to send it to the advocacy team right away.
  • Stop-loss that fits. The stop-loss policy has to reimburse claims paid at the RBP level.
  • The No Surprises Act has limits. It protects members for emergency care and air ambulance. It does not cover scheduled care with an out-of-network provider.
