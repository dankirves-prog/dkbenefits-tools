Step 2 ERISA Scope & Fiduciaries
This version is for private-employer ERISA welfare plans only. Fiduciary roles must be explicitly identified.
Scope limitation Governmental plans, church or church-related plans, and arrangements of uncertain ERISA status are not supported in this version. If your plan falls into one of those categories, stop here.
ERISA Applicability
Not supported in this version This tool only generates documents for private-employer ERISA welfare plans. Please use a qualified ERISA attorney or a different tool for your plan type.
Plan Administrator ? The Plan Administrator is the person or entity responsible for operating the plan. For most small to mid-size employers this is the employer itself, represented by an HR director, owner, or officer.
Named Fiduciary ? ERISA requires every plan to name at least one fiduciary with authority to control and manage the plan. This is usually the same person or entity as the Plan Administrator.
Agent for Service of Legal Process ? The person or entity who can accept legal process on behalf of the plan. Required by ERISA. This is often the same as the employer or the Plan Administrator.
If left blank, the document will list the employer's business address as the address for service of legal process on the agent. Enter a separate address only if the agent operates from a different location (e.g. an attorney's office, a registered-agent service, or a corporate officer at a different site).
Discretionary Authority
What this means A discretionary authority clause gives the Plan Administrator authority to interpret plan terms and determine eligibility. Under ERISA case law this can change the standard courts apply when reviewing benefit decisions. However, for fully insured plans, state insurance laws in some states limit or prohibit discretionary clauses in insured policies, which can reduce the practical effect. Including the clause is generally recommended but is not universally effective for all insured benefits.
