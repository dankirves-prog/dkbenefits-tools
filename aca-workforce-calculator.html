ACA Workforce Size Calculator

Employers often call this ATNE, the average total number of employees. That is full-time employees plus full-time equivalents.

This estimates applicable large employer status for a calendar year from the prior year's workforce. It is an educational estimate for Florida and Georgia employers. It is not legal, tax, or accounting advice.

How the count works

  1. For each month, count full-time employees. Full-time means an average of at least 30 hours of service a week, or 130 hours in the month.
  2. Add full-time equivalents. Take hours of service for everyone who is not full-time, count no more than 120 hours for any one person, and divide by 120.
  3. Add the 12 monthly totals, divide by 12, and round down to the next lowest whole number. That whole number is the ATNE used for the 50-employee line.
  4. 50 or more means applicable large employer status for the next calendar year, unless the seasonal worker exception applies.

How should we read the workforce?

Each person is capped at 120 hours in the month before the hours are divided by 120. Leave non-full-time blank if everyone is full-time.

2. Seasonal worker exception

If the workforce exceeds 50 for 120 days or fewer, and the employees in excess of 50 during that stretch are seasonal workers, the employer is not an applicable large employer. Four calendar months may be treated as 120 days. Those months do not have to be consecutive.

3. Rules that change who is counted

Add the related companies together first. Use 0 if they have no employees. This is not an ownership quiz.

TRICARE and VA coverage. An employee is left out of the 50-employee count for a month in which the employee has medical coverage through the military, including TRICARE, or through the Veterans Administration. Leave those employees out of the numbers you type. This exclusion is only for applicable large employer status.
